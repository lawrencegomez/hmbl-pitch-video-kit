# What Lawrence likes (read before the concepts and the storyboard)

Learned from four rounds on one startup video in full bright Vox style (2026-10-16). His verdicts, in his terms,
with what to do about each. No company names here: this file is shared with every recruiter's kit.

## Overall
- **Bright, busy, exciting.** A startup explainer should excite a candidate. A grey, print-only Vox version was "too
  dark and gloomy" and "tried too hard to make everything the same few Vox style types". Pastel paper per scene, many
  small moving parts, one text animation at a time. (PLAYBOOK "Bright Vox".)
- **Fresh beats remade.** When asked for a new version, write a new script and new visuals; don't re-skin the old one.
- **Length:** 41 s was fine "since the user's attention is grabbed throughout". Motion in every second matters more
  than hitting 40 s.
- **The bar:** "really great" came from evidence on screen (real posts, real product, real headline) plus playful
  cut-paper motion, all timed to the words.

## Hook
- **Liked:** opening on the company's own bold claim from its launch post ("<old way> is dead!"), with the words
  highlighted as they are said, a red strike-through, and a big bold stamp on the charged word.
- **Disliked:** a definition as the opener ("<jargon> means ..."), and gimmick stickers ("HOT TAKE" looked weird).
- **Voice:** the bold claim read serious and matter of fact (steadier take, slightly faster), then the normal upbeat
  read from the next scene on. He asked for this after an excited read of the claim "sounded weird".
- **Clean hero objects:** a small envelope half-covering the stamp "looked weird". Make the key object big and central
  (one email grows in the middle of the screen, then bursts into a spray to everyone).

## What the company does
- **Liked:** a crowd of paper people, the camera diving into one person, a radar sweep, signal chips, "New job" lighting
  up, a note landing. Keep this kind of "show the mechanism" beat.
- **Product screens:** a small screenshot read as "a little email whatever it is" and didn't look like the product
  category. Show their real product UI big and readable, push the camera in, and highlight the rows that prove the
  claim. If it can't be made readable, cut it.
- **No logo card** when the hook already showed the name and logo.

## Founders
- **Tell the story, not every fact.** "Repeat founders who sold their last startup to <buyer>, then came back to YC"
  beat "won <customer> and <customer> with cold outreach" ("weird and not needed for this story").
- **Third-party press is good evidence:** a news headline torn out big, with the key words highlighted as said.
- **Stamps:** make them bold and big (double border, dense ink, slam with a shake and impact lines). Thin, faded
  stamps read as weak.

## Traction
- **Revenue as speed and trajectory:** launch month, the first big milestone, and where it is heading, in the
  source's words. A to-scale bar chart where the camera zooms out to reveal a dashed goal bar worked.
- **Liked as is:** a tile grid for "40+ customers", cash stacking for the raise.

## Office map
- **Zoom in close** to the neighbourhood (city, then blocks, then the street) and **highlight the area in colour**
  (yellow fill, red outline, label). A city-level map was not enough.

## Music
- **Keep one feel the whole way.** A bed that "starts off cool but then sounds different" (a louder, brighter section
  partway through) was rejected. Prompt for one steady groove; check the bed for section changes and loop the opening
  bars if it shifts (PLAYBOOK "Music").

## Working with him
- **Approve the plan first:** hook options, script and a section-by-section plan before anything is spent (SKILL step 4).
  Small, exact fixes he spells out go straight to the build.
- **Speed matters.** He asked why edits take long. Keep revision rounds lean: own frame checks for small edits, the
  two outside reviewers only for a new video or big changes; offer the Remotion Studio live preview for tweak rounds.
- **Getting the video to him:** send it as a playable card (it reaches his phone), give a localhost link, and when he
  wants to keep it, copy the final to `~/Downloads/<Company> pitch video.mp4`.
- **Let him hear choices:** for a voice-tone change, record two short takes and let him pick.
