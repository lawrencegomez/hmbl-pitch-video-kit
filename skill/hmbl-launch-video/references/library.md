## The launch bar (what the great ones share)

1. **One world, one accent.** One canvas the whole way (white, dark, or a brand paper) and one accent colour. Scenes
   change by motion inside that world, not by cutting away. 5 of 9 have three or fewer hard cuts in 50 to 85 s.
2. **Words carry the script.** 7 of 9 have no narrator. Each line is one idea in 3 to 9 words ("It runs your Apple
   Ads." "And pauses what wastes money."), built word by word, held long enough to read.
3. **Every feature = one line + one proof.** The line names it; a real product screen right under it proves it (a row
   turning to "Paused", a price list sorting, a notification arriving). No abstract filler.
4. **One real task, start to finish.** Find the cheapest PS5 across four agents; talk a $287 bill down; plan a Porto
   trip.
5. **Numbers move.** Slot counters, bars turning green, a price line dropping, 1,200 hotels narrowing to 1.
6. **Abundance shots.** Walls of real UI behind a number, icon walls, card collages, face grids.
7. **One gag.** "Nobody bought anything." Mirrored text. "Tinder but for dogs." It makes it shareable.
8. **Scale contrast.** A giant line that runs off the frame, then a tiny "In any app."
9. **Music is a steady bed.** Fades in over 3 to 8 s, holds, fades at the logo. Only Selfdrive locks motion to the
   beat; the rest pace to reading speed. Beat-locking is a style choice, not a rule.
10. **The end is an offer.** Logo lockup ("The all-new [icon] wabi app") plus the URL, price, date, platforms or free
    credits. Unlike pitch videos, launches ask for action.

**Shape that fits all nine:** promise (what it is in one line) -> one real task through the product -> 3 to 6 feature
beats (line + proof) -> proof (numbers, logos, faces) -> where and how much -> logo + URL.
**Lengths in practice:** 20 to 30 s (an offer), 50 to 67 s (the main launch), 80 to 90 s (YouTube walkthrough, often
narrated).

## Story orders (pick one per launch)

Adapted from HeyGen's launch skill, in our words, and matched to the nine. The order is the spine; the shape above
(promise -> task -> features -> proof -> offer) fits inside any of them. Build around the one promise, never the order
of their website.

| Order | Use when | Beats | Which of the nine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem, then fix | the pain is known and sharp | hook -> the pain -> why it hurts -> the product -> it working -> offer | Letra (every case you have retyped, then "Now it's real text") |
| Imagine the future | a new category or a new way of living | "imagine" -> name it -> the pain gone -> how it works -> the outcome -> offer | Interface (people talking to a ring), Kylon (the band metaphor) |
| Show it working | the product explains itself on screen | a question -> the product -> one task, start to finish -> a second task -> trust -> offer | Offhand (one task across four agents), Wabi (a trip planned, a bill talked down) |
| Before and after | it replaces an old way of doing something | before -> a glimpse of after -> the product -> step 1 -> step 2 -> the wow -> offer | Ollie (the morning crisis, averted), Odessia ("Same trip. 40% less.") |
| Feature, then benefit | many features, or a status product | a category hook -> feature -> what it gives you -> feature -> what it gives you -> climax -> offer | Selfdrive ("It runs your Apple Ads. / And pauses what wastes money."), Photon (number after number) |

With many features, every feature still turns into what the viewer gets; never a stack of raw features.

## Hook types (the first 3 to 5 seconds)

One hook, built to create tension, curiosity or want. Never open on a plain company description.
- **A category announcement:** "Introducing Photon 2.0", "a new kind of messenger" (Photon, Wabi).
- **A promise said straight to the viewer:** "This agent will get users for your app while you sleep." (Selfdrive)
- **A visual spectacle:** Letra's floating collage, Wabi's glossy 3D world.
- **"Imagine" / a moment from the future:** Interface's everyday voice commands.
- **A startling number, counting up alone,** before any product is shown.
- **Naming the pain:** a line the viewer has thought themselves (our example, not from the nine: "Still retyping that
  parcel number?").
- **A question,** often typed live with a caret.

## More moves to build when a launch needs them

From HeyGen's launch skill (in our words), beyond what the nine show. None are built yet; add each to `parts.tsx`
as a shared part the first time a launch uses it, then mark it here.

| Move | Best for | The line it carries |
|---|---|---|
| A prompt types itself, is sent, and the result generates | an AI product's intro or a feature | the ask itself is the line |
| Options cycle in one slot, then the real answer crashes in | a hook or the product intro | "A doc? A database? A wiki? It's all three." |
| A typed line deletes a word and retypes a better one | a hook | a thought the viewer has had, then reframed |
| An agent at work: the task starts, rows tick off, a receipt lands | a feature for an agent product | "It checks every file, flags what broke, fixes what it can." |
| Tools and tabs pile up until they bury the screen | the problem beat | "Slack, email, docs, tickets, and it all lands on you." |
| Partner logos orbit the product at the centre | proof, integrations | "Works with everything you already use." |
| Two panels side by side | a pair of features, or before and after | "Design on the left, code on the right." |
| A number that grows bigger as it counts | proof | one number, said as it lands |
| The website as a tilted card that scrolls | the intro for a web product | a real full-page screenshot, never a rebuild |
| Close on a mystery detail, then pull back to the whole workspace | a hook | the answer lands with the pull-back |
| A small widget changes shape and does its trick | a hook or the offer | "It starts as a search bar. It becomes your workflow." |
| A wall of feature tiles assembles at once | breadth of features | "look how much it does" |
| The key word glows as it is said | emphasis anywhere | one word per line at most |
| The logo assembles with no words | the product intro or the outro | just the name |

## Code vs Higgsfield vs a real shoot

| Element | How | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic type, product stage (phone, browser, chat, cursor, tables), data, walls, bookends | code (the kit) + their real screenshots or screen recordings | free |
| Inline icons and props | real emoji (Apple's on a Mac); Microsoft Fluent 3D emoji (MIT) when a client needs zero licence risk; the product's real app icon | free |
| Glossy abstract 3D openers and closers | Higgsfield still + Seedance clip | ~40 credits a clip |
| Fabric or curtain reveal | Higgsfield Seedance or stock footage | ~35 credits |
| Narrator (80 to 90 s YouTube cut only) | Maya, or his ElevenLabs | under 1 credit |
| Music bed | Sonilo, instrumental, steady | ~2.25 credits |
| A song with vocals (Odessia) | licensed track (Artlist, Musicbed) | subscription |
| Creator or customer faces | real people's footage or photos, never generated | n/a |
| Live-action films (Kylon, Interface) | a film shoot; AI can make lifestyle B-roll but never the real product; ~12 clips, 400+ credits, hard consistency | high |

## The nine, beat by beat

**1. Wabi 2.0** (80 s, 4K 60 fps). 0-6 glossy 3D dusk world, "Introducing", typewriter "a new kind of messenger" ->
"that makes apps" (gradient text) · 6-16 white canvas, "and gets things done", 3D props fly out, a chat builds a real
trip plan from a flight email, hearts float · 17-29 "Add your friends" (real faces pop around the word), group
planning, maps, "Life admin? Handled." · 30-39 a bill negotiated down from $287, "the right outcome / in the right
interface" · 40-56 the word wheel (mini-apps, trackers, games, experiments), an app made in chat (a pet donkey) · 56-68
"Some apps last a day / Some grow with you", a curved carousel of app icons, "A home screen that's truly yours" · 68-80
"iOS. Android.", 3D bookend, "The all-new [icon] wabi app", wabi.ai.
Why it works: calm Apple pace, one idea a beat, the product does specific real jobs on screen; the 3D bookends make it
feel premium and the white canvas lets the UI pop. Fun: faces popping around "friends", hearts, the donkey app.

**2. Photon 2.0** (22 s). "Introducing Photon 2.0" (blue "2.0") · a glossy pill button clicked by a cursor, "With just
one click" · "Your agent is everywhere", channel list with real icons · "And it's free for everyone" (zoom-through) ·
10,000+ SMS, 100,000+ emails, 8000+ voice minutes, unlimited iMessage / WhatsApp / Telegram, each over a wall of that
channel's real UI · "Even 10DLC registration" with a check · "Try it today at photon.codes/beta" · logo.
Why it works: an offer cut to 22 s, every beat a number or a channel; walls of UI say "abundance" without a word.
Fun: the slot digits and the rush through the words.

**3. Ollie** (83 s, narrated). Mascot logo intro · "Let me show you how it works": add Ollie to the family group chat,
connect email, calendar, WhatsApp (real screens) · "deploys an army of agents" (coloured triangles fly across) ·
tomorrow's schedule, a dentist reminder, a fridge photo becomes a dinner plan · dark section "While you sleep, Ollie is
constantly monitoring": a medical bill, a soccer camp deadline, a delayed flight stamped red, carpool, a moved Zoom ·
"Your morning crisis is averted" · "Unlike other AI chat bots, Ollie is a proactive partner" · a mosaic of family
photos, social proof · logo.
Why it works: a day-in-the-life with real screens and captions, then proof. Best for YouTube, where people watch 80 s.
Fun: the triangle agent army, the red stamp on the delayed flight.

**4. Letra** (56 s). Floating collage, "This is Letra" types, app icon · "Copy any text on your screen.", the menu bar
app opens, "Lives in your menu bar." (serif italic "Always one shortcut away.") · a fast montage of real cases: a Zoom
screen share, a paused video, a parcel label, screenshots, photos · a mirrored "Q3 roadmap" gag · "Now it's real
text." -> the giant "Paste it anywhere" -> tiny "In any app." · "Languages. QR codes. Links.", "Works offline" ·
"Everything on your screen, copyable." explodes into a collage · logo + URL.
Why it works: you recognise every case (everyone has retyped a parcel number), so it lands as "I need that". The film
sits in a white card on a black frame, like a Mac window. Fun: the mirrored text, the explosion.

**5. Offhand** (67 s). "One MCP connects all your agents" with inline icons, the "your _ agents" slot cycling through
mascots · "Start in any chat." · a request typed and sent to all agents · a status log, then a price table where four
agents report and the rows sort · "All four agents and I reported. Nobody bought anything." · "Let one agent hand out
tasks.", a cursor drags agents into a tree, "The others get just the task." · "Build your agent loop", rounds 1, 2, 3
of a design improving with comments · "Set it up in one minute. No computer needed." · icon wall · logo.
Why it works: the mechanism told as one funny, concrete task; mascots make abstract agents feel like characters.
Fun: "Nobody bought anything."

**6. Odessia** (85 s). Floating hotel photos, "Odessia finds the flights / books the hotel / gets the tickets / makes
each day perfect" (word swaps with inline icons) · a text conversation, "Send a text. Get a trip back." · itinerary
screens · "Assembles your preferences from your inbox" · the dot field narrowing to one hotel and its score · "Get
discounted rates" (bars), "+32,400" points · "Better than a luxury card. None of the fees." with a red velvet curtain
opening on "The Odessia Collection" · navy night: "Your concierge by day. A travel hacker by night." · "Same trip. 40%
less." (bars turn green) · flight arcs on a dot map · "Prices drop. You're rebooked." (price line falls) · circle wipe,
"Text Odessia to get started for free.", the URL.
Why it works: serif type, cream paper and real hotel photos read as luxury, and every claim is proved with a number;
the vocal song gives it feeling. Fun: the curtain, the dot field.

**7. Kylon** (67 s, live action). A conductor ("Where's my baton?") leads a jazz band; each player wears a patch
(OpenAI, Claude, GitHub, "CEO", "Marketing Lead", "Non-technical co-founder"); the baton is engraved Kylon; it all
comes together; logo over the wide stage, "better together".
Why it works: one strong metaphor, cinematic warmth, brand only at the end. Memorable, but explains very little.
Needs a film shoot.

**8. Selfdrive** (50 s, beat-locked, dark). "This agent" inside a giant app-icon square · "will get users for your
app" with channel tags · "while you sleep." · paste your App Store link · "Go to sleep, human." with a terminal-style
task list · a lock-screen notification, "You got 50 new downloads", Approve · "You're still the boss. It just does the
work." · the ladder: "It runs your Apple Ads. / And pauses what wastes money." · "It rewrites your App Store page. /
You tap Approve." · "It builds your website. / And gets you into ChatGPT answers." · "It manages real human creators. /
You never send a single DM." (a grid of creator faces) · "It reads your reviews. / And ships what users ask for." ·
"An app builder. A marketing team." · "Superapp, the self-driving app company."
Why it works: one clear promise, then a ladder of features each proved by a screen, cut tight to the beat; "You tap
Approve" keeps the viewer in control. Fun: "Go to sleep, human."

**9. Interface** (90 s, 24 fps, live action). People in everyday moments (a commute, a beach, a laundromat, a diner, a
shower, a bike ride, a concert) speak to a ring on their finger: "Order my usual from the corner store and add a bottle
of red." "Start the dishwasher." "Next startup idea: Tinder but for dogs." Small chat bubbles float beside them · a
macro shot of the ring: "Introducing Interface, for the agent era. Join the waitlist."
Why it works: the voice commands are the script and the jokes; it sells a lifestyle around a physical product.
Needs the real hardware and a film shoot; AI cannot stand in for the product.

## Which reference fits which launch

- **Short offer, pricing or a free tier, 20 to 30 s:** Photon (numbers over UI walls, zoom-through, URL at the end).
- **Software with several features, 50 to 67 s:** Selfdrive (line ladder + proof screens) or Offhand (one funny task,
  inline icons, a table that sorts).
- **A consumer app, premium feel:** Wabi (white canvas, chat that builds, word wheel, 3D bookends).
- **A utility people instantly recognise:** Letra (montage of real cases, giant line then tiny line).
- **Luxury or lifestyle service, every claim a number:** Odessia (cream serif, dot field, bars, curtain).
- **YouTube walkthrough, 80 to 90 s:** Ollie (narrated day in the life, real screens).
- **Hardware, or a brand film with budget:** Kylon or Interface (needs a shoot; say so up front).
